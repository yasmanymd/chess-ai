## Objective

Elicit and document testable functional and non-functional requirements for the first product release, consistent with the accepted Project Charter and Human–AI Working Agreement.

## Context

The accepted MVP scope covers temporary player identities, creation and joining of two-player games, server-authoritative chess rules and state, real-time synchronization, reconnection, persistence, replay, PGN import and export, English, Spanish, and French interfaces, public deployment, and public engineering documentation.

This session will refine that scope into explicit requirements, business rules, edge cases, and acceptance criteria. It will not select the architecture or technology stack.

## Interview Plan

1. Player identity and entry experience.
2. Game creation, discovery, and joining.
3. Chess gameplay and clocks.
4. Disconnection, reconnection, and abandonment.
5. Game completion, persistence, history, and replay.
6. PGN interoperability.
7. Internationalization and accessibility.
8. Security, privacy, performance, and operational expectations.
9. MVP acceptance scenarios and requirement approval.

## Later Amendment

Session 007 verified FIDE articles 6.9 and 9.2–9.6 and found that the phrase "standard automatic draw conditions" in S002-D13 was imprecise. Product Requirements 1.1 supersedes only that interpretation: threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule are claim-based, while fivefold repetition and the seventy-five-move rule are automatic. Dead-position and timeout semantics were also made more precise.
